Support & maintenance proposal · Zyphex Tech

Keeping Baseline Breaker in play

A published game needs someone watching the stores, the operating systems and the numbers. This proposal sets out three retainer levels, what each covers, how requests are handled, and what is charged separately.

1. Why a retainer

  • The stores move. Apple and Google raise minimum SDK and policy requirements every year; an app that is not updated is eventually delisted.
  • Operating systems move. New iOS and Android releases each autumn change rendering, permissions and audio behaviour. We test on betas before release day.
  • Players write reviews. Prompt replies and fast fixes keep the rating up, and the rating drives installs.
  • The game should keep improving. A steady cadence of small updates keeps players returning and gives the brand something to announce.

4. How it works

  • Requests go to a shared board; each is acknowledged within the response time of the plan and given a size and a target release.
  • Releases ship on a fixed monthly date for Rally and Tournament, with hotfixes at any time for crashes or store issues.
  • Severity. Blocker (app unusable or delisted): work starts immediately. Major (a feature broken for many players): next business day. Minor: next scheduled release.
  • Reporting. A one-page monthly summary: downloads, active players, retention, crash-free rate, rating, work done, work planned.
  • Term. Three-month minimum, then monthly; 30 days' notice; level can change at any month boundary.
